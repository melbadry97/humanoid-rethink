### Recommendation: a small team doing morphology + controller co-design in the cloud without a big NVIDIA GPU

**Pick: the MuJoCo ecosystem. Use MJX + MuJoCo Playground as the core, MuJoCo CPU for sim2sim checks, and keep the option to switch the same MJCF models to MuJoCo Warp / mjlab when an NVIDIA GPU is available.**

Why:
1. **Hardware fit.** MJX runs on Google Cloud TPUs, AMD GPUs, Apple Silicon and small NVIDIA GPUs ([MJX docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html)). Isaac Sim, by contrast, needs an RTX-class GPU of at least RTX 4080 / 16 GB and explicitly does not support A100 or H100 ([requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/installation/requirements.html)).
2. **Morphology edits are cheap.** A body is a plain MJCF XML text file that an LLM can edit. MJX is **differentiable**, which helps gradient-based design-parameter search ([MJX docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html)). MJWarp and Newton are not differentiable yet ([MJWarp docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.5.0/mjwarp/index.html)).
3. **Humanoids work out of the box with real sim-to-real.** Playground has G1, H1, T1, Berkeley Humanoid and OP3, and transferred zero-shot on the G1, T1 and Berkeley Humanoid ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08844v1)).
4. **Popularity and momentum.** MuJoCo has 15.7k stars. Brax now defers to Playground ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/brax/)). MJWarp is co-developed with NVIDIA and is the default solver inside Newton and Isaac Lab 3.0 ([Isaac Lab notes](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/release/3.0.0/source/refs/release_notes.html)), so models and skills carry over.
5. **Fallback if the team has no NVIDIA or TPU hardware at all.** Genesis runs on AMD, Metal and CPU, but its benchmarks are contested ([aiwiki](https://www.aiwiki.ai/wiki/genesis_simulator)).

Caveats:
- MJX-JAX slows down in contact-heavy scenes ([MJX docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html)).
- Recent LLM co-design papers use small benchmark bodies (Ant, Walker) in MuJoCo or Brax rather than full humanoids (RoboMoRe, Debate2Create, see below).

### LLM-written rewards and simulation configurations

- **Eureka** (ICLR 2024): GPT-4 writes and evolves reward code. Tested on 29 environments and 10 morphologies, it beat expert rewards on about 83% of tasks with roughly 52% average normalized gain. Demonstrated Shadow Hand pen spinning ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12931v2)). Repo has 3,212 stars ([repo](https://github.com/eureka-research/Eureka)).
- **DrEureka** (RSS 2024): the LLM writes both the reward and the domain-randomization ranges for sim-to-real. Results are competitive with human-designed configurations. A quadruped balanced on a yoga ball ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01967v1)). Repo has 947 stars ([repo](https://github.com/eureka-research/DrEureka)).
- **ARS: Agentic Reward System** (2026): VLM agents estimate per-frame task progress as a reward, with verification. Used on a real robot for multi-screw fastening ([arXiv 2609.34484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34484)).

### LLM-designed robots (morphology)

- **RoboGen** (2023): a propose → generate scene → learn loop. Picks between RL, motion planning and trajectory optimization ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01455v1)). 1,230 stars ([repo](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/RoboGen)).
- **Text2Robot** (Duke, ICRA 2025): text → 3D model → manufacturable robot. Evolution and RL co-optimize body and gait. Walking in simulation within about an hour; a printed and assembled quadruped in under a day ([Duke](https://pratt.duke.edu/news/text2robot/)).
- **RoboMorph** (ICLR-W 2025): an LLM acts as the mutation operator over a modular-robot grammar. Matches or beats RoboGrammar on 4 terrains ([site](https://robomorph.github.io/), [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclrw/2025/qiu2025iclrw-robomorph/)).
- **RoboMoRe** (May 2025): the LLM jointly evolves **morphology and reward** in MuJoCo (Ant, Hopper, Walker and others). Reported efficiency gains are large, but a reviewer flags comparability issues ([Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2506.00276)).
- **Debate2Create** (ICML 2026): design and control agents debate the body, write JAX reward code and train in **Brax**. Reported 3.2x over the default Ant and uses 36% fewer candidates than RoboMoRe ([Lacuna](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/debate2create-robot-co-design-via-multi-agent-llm)).
- **LASeR** (LLM-aided evolutionary search) and Stella et al. (conversational design), cited in Cornell's "LLMs for Model-Based Robot Design" (2026). That paper found that **direct LLM component selection produced no verifiably feasible designs**, while LLM-drafted models plus a constraint solver did ([arXiv 2609.33423](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33423v1)).
- **Non-LLM baseline worth knowing:** "Accelerated Co-Design via Morphological Pretraining" (Strgar & Kriegman, ICLR 2026). A universal controller pretrained over 10M bodies in a differentiable Taichi simulator, followed by evolution, avoided diversity collapse ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0cfeabacfcdf49ce45826a2f7eb89a3c-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### Agentic coding loops for robotics

- **Code as Policies** (Google, 2022): the LLM writes robot policy code directly. This is the precursor of the current loops ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.07753), [Google blog](https://research.google/blog/robots-that-write-their-own-code/)).
- **Anthropic Project Fetch.**
  - Phase 1 (Aug 2025, Opus 4.1): non-experts using Claude programmed a robot dog faster than those without it.
  - Phase 2 (Jun 18 2026, **Opus 4.7, autonomous**): at least 10x faster than human teams on every task they had completed, and about 20x faster than the fastest team overall. Wrote 1,045 lines of code. Limits: it struggled with fine ball nudging, and a human finished the fetch program ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-fetch-phase-two)).
- **HARBOR** (Jun 2026): a multi-agent harness that takes a simulator codebase and a task through environment setup, reward design, hyperparameter tuning and a trained policy. Tested on 6 benchmarks and 16 tasks, with real transfer claimed ([arXiv 2606.08610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08610)).
- **ENPIRE** (NVIDIA/CMU/Berkeley, Jun 2026): coding agents improve **real-robot** policies on 8 bimanual YAM stations coordinated through Git. Reached 99% success on Push-T, pin insertion and zip-tie cutting. On RoboCasa it beat GR00T ([arXiv 2606.19980](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19980)).
- **Also seen:** EvoTrainer, co-evolving policies and training harnesses ([arXiv 2606.03108](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03108v1)); SR-Platform, natural-language → simulation environment synthesis ([arXiv 2605.14700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14700)); and LLM-driven autonomous real-world RL ([arXiv 2503.04280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04280)). These were not read in depth.

### Overall picture (late 2026)

1. **Hierarchical "brain + cerebellum" stacks are standard.** Helix uses S2/S1 ([Figure](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix)), GR00T a System 2/1 split ([aiwiki](https://www.aiwiki.ai/wiki/isaac_gr00t/raw)), and Gemini splits ER planning from the VLA ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-15-brings-ai-agents-into-the-physical-world/)).
2. **RL-trained low-level humanoid controllers in GPU simulators remain the base layer** (Playground, Isaac Lab, mjlab).
3. **LLM agents increasingly automate the engineering around robots** rather than the motor control itself: rewards, domain randomization, environments, debugging and morphology. Examples are Eureka, DrEureka, HARBOR, ENPIRE and Project Fetch.
4. **For morphology specifically, LLM-in-the-loop evolution is promising but early.** The bodies are small (Ant, Walker, modular grammars). Comparisons are noisy ([Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2506.00276)), and direct LLM design without a verifier fails ([arXiv 2609.33423](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33423v1)).

### 1.2 Wheeled-legged hybrids and humanoid alternatives
- **ANYmal on wheels (ETH, which spun out Swiss-Mile):** reaches 4 m/s with an **83% lower cost of transport** than legged-only locomotion ([arXiv 1809.03557](https://arxiv.com/abs/1809.03557)).
- **Unitree Go2-W:** 16 joint motors at ~45 N·m, 7" pneumatic tires, 0–2.5 m/s, 35° climbing angle, 18 kg ([Generation Robots](https://www.generationrobots.com/en/404401-go2-w-wheeled-quadruped-robot.html)).
- **Galbot G1:** omnidirectional base, 47 DoF, a **65 cm vertical torso lift** that reaches from the floor to ~2.4 m, 4–8 h runtime, and it cannot fall over. The trade-off is no stairs ([aiwiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/galbot_g1)).
- **Industry shift:** of more than 90 humanoid-style releases in H1 2026, **~48% were wheeled and ~45% bipedal**, the first time wheeled designs outnumbered bipeds. At WAIC 2026, Galbot S1 stacked 30 kg crates ([TMTPost](https://www.tmtpost.com/8096593.html)).
- **RAI Institute Roadrunner:** a biped with inline wheels whose symmetric legs let the knees bend either way. It climbs stairs and balances on one wheel ([Laughing Squid](https://laughingsquid.com/roadrunner-bipedal-robot/)).
- **BD Stretch:** omni base and a 7-DoF arm, 800 cases/h of ~50 lb boxes, lasts a full shift ([Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/21354619/)).
- **Hello Robot Stretch 3:** 24.5 kg, $24,950, a mast-and-telescope home manipulator ([Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/stretch-3-mobile-manipulator-hello-robot-designed-open-source-development/)).

### 1.3 Cross-cutting lessons
1. **Falls are a design requirement, not an edge case.** DRC teams found Atlas "too top-heavy and its arms too weak to get up reliably." Most humanoids fell in uncontrolled tests. Robots also avoided using railings and walls for support ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/topics-resources/content/5-lessons-darpa-robotics-challenge)). At the 2025 Beijing half-marathon (21 entrants), robots fell at the start and handlers had to hold some up. The winner, Tiangong Ultra, took 2:40 and **3 battery swaps** ([Malay Mail](https://malaymail.com/news/life/2025/04/19/china-showcases-robot-runners-in-beijing-half-marathon-some-fall-flat-while-others-finish-strong/173659)).
2. **Energy is the binding constraint.** Human CoT is ≈0.20. The Cornell passive biped also reaches 0.20, while ASIMO was 3.23 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_dynamics)). Cassie measured 0.762 and H1 0.718. The 10.5 kg Wow Orin, with co-optimized leg lengths, reached 0.407 ([SERL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15632)). In MIT Cheetah, about 76% of energy was lost as motor heat, giving CoT 0.5 at 6 m/s ([Seok et al.](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/108096)). Runtimes are still around 1–4 h.
3. **Hands and forearms are the reliability bottleneck.** Evidence: the Optimus production stall, the Figure 02 forearm failures above, and arm loads of only 2–3 kg on G1.
4. **The actuator trade space is converging.** The pattern is QDD (≤10:1) in legs for impact tolerance and current-based torque sensing, and high reductions in arms, for example the 9:1 Berkeley Humanoid and 6:1 Mini Cheetah. QDD's weakness is heat while holding static loads ([humanoid.guide QDD](https://humanoid.guide/?p=18213)). Production humanoids mostly skip VSAs because of cost and mass ([humanoid.guide VSA](https://humanoid.guide/?p=19557)).
5. **Morphology iteration is slow and manual.** One example is H1→H1-2 taking more than a year ([arXiv 2510.03081](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03081v1)). Automated co-design is the opening this report targets.

**Tooling:** MuJoCo Playground (MJX) trains G1 and Booster T1 locomotion in under 30 min on 2× RTX 4090 and Berkeley Humanoid in under 15 min. G1 and T1 transferred zero-shot to indoor floors. JIT compilation costs 1–3 min, and contact cost scales with possible contacts, not active ones ([arXiv 2502.08844](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08844v1)). That is fast enough for an inner loop that scores tens of designs per GPU-day.

**[H] Proposed recursive loop:**
1. An LLM proposer (LASeR-style reflection) edits MJCF parameters inside a bounded grammar.
2. A morphology-conditioned URMA/MetaMorph policy is fine-tuned per candidate in MJX, so no candidate is trained from scratch.
3. Each candidate gets a multi-objective score: CoT, recovery rate, task success, and an actuator-envelope check following GLAMDRING.
4. A MAP-Elites archive keeps diversity across niches (home, warehouse, rubble).
5. The proposer reads the archive, then writes and tests a new hypothesis.
6. Every N generations, the proposer's own prompts and grammar are updated from what won.

## Section 4: What's next — 10 ranked design hypotheses

Ranking reflects expected impact on agility and usefulness across homes, warehouses, and disaster response, multiplied by how cheaply each can be tested in MuJoCo/MJX. The baseline for all is a **Unitree G1 or Booster T1 MJCF from MuJoCo Playground**, trained with PPO under a URMA/MetaMorph-style morphology-conditioned policy. Each design gets ≥3 seeds, with domain randomization on mass, friction, latency, and motor strength. Every hypothesis below is **[H]**.

| # | Hypothesis | MuJoCo test | Primary metric (secondary) | Pass threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Wheel-leg hybrid feet** (driven wheel at the ankle that locks into a foot) give most of the 83% CoT win on flat ground ([ANYmal-W](https://arxiv.com/abs/1809.03557)) and keep stairs. This aligns with the 2026 wheeled shift ([TMTPost](https://www.tmtpost.com/8096593.html)) | Add a hinge plus a cylinder geom at each foot with a lock equality. Tasks: 50 m flat, 15 cm stairs, 20 cm curb, rubble field | CoT on flat (stair success %, mode-switch time) | CoT ≤ 50% of legged baseline, stair success ≥ 90% of baseline |
| **2** | **Fall-first morphology:** a lower CoM, stronger shoulders and arms, and rounded padded shells raise fall recovery and survivability (DRC lesson; RoboCraft's ≥40% gain from morphology) | Run RoboCraft-style search on CoM height, arm torque limit, and shell geometry. | **3** | **Clutched parallel ankle and knee springs** cut actuator heat and energy while walking and holding loads (exo 7.2% result; QDD heat issue) | Spring as a joint `stiffness` gated by an equality or weld enabled in stance, with policy-controlled clutch actions | ∑τ²·dt per meter, a proxy for copper loss (CoT) | −15% ∑τ² at 1.2 m/s |

**Evaluation protocol (for the self-improvement loop):**
- **Multi-objective vector:** CoT at 1.2 m/s, max speed, get-up %, push recovery at 50 ms latency, shelf reach coverage, stair %, and ∑τ² hold-heat proxy.
- **Constraints:** total mass ≤ 40 kg, height 1.2–1.7 m, every joint inside a real actuator's torque–speed envelope (per GLAMDRING, envelope feasibility determines realizable payload), and BOM/part-count proxies.
- **Archive:** MAP-Elites niches for home, warehouse, and rubble.
- **Guard:** never accept a design on an LLM's judgement alone, since off-the-shelf LLMs struggle to separate designs with small performance differences ([RoboCrafter-QA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02249v2)). Validate the top designs with ≥5 seeds and a held-out terrain set before promoting them.
